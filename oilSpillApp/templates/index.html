Oil Spill Detection System

An oil spill is the release of liquid petroleum hydrocarbons into the environment, particularly into the marine ecosystem, due to human activity. Oil spills are among the most destructive forms of pollution, affecting marine life, coastal ecosystems, and local economies dependent on fisheries and tourism.

Oil Spill Awareness & Management

Causes of Oil Spills

Causes of Oil Spills

A. Operational Causes:
- Pipeline leaks or ruptures: Corrosion, poor maintenance, or natural disasters.
- Tanker accidents: Collisions, groundings, or structural failures.
- Offshore drilling accidents: Blowouts or mechanical failures.
- Loading/unloading operations: Spillage during transfer.

B. Human Errors:
- Mismanagement or inadequate training.
- Overfilling tanks or improper monitoring.
- Delayed response or communication failures.

C. Natural Causes:
- Earthquakes, hurricanes, storms.
- Seafloor movements affecting pipelines or storage.

Prevention of Oil Spills

Prevention of Oil Spills

A. Engineering & Design:
- Double-hulled tankers.
- Automated monitoring systems.
- Corrosion control and maintenance.
- Blowout preventers.

B. Operational & Management:
- Routine inspections.
- Strict loading/unloading protocols.
- Employee training.
- Environmental audits and risk assessments.

C. Legal & Policy:
- MARPOL, OPRC, Clean Water Act.
- Mandatory contingency plans.

Handling & Response Methods

Handling & Response Methods

A. Containment & Recovery: Booms, Skimmers, Sorbents.
B. Chemical Methods: Dispersants, Solidifiers, Surface washing agents.
C. Biological Methods: Microorganisms, Nutrient enrichment, Phytoremediation.
D. In-Situ Burning: Controlled burning of oil slicks.
E. Remote Sensing & Detection: Satellites, drones, radar, AI-based monitoring.
